LokalBot vs OpenWhispr

OpenWhispr is a popular open-source app for dictation and meeting notes, under the MIT license. Like LokalBot, it records your microphone and system audio without a bot and can transcribe and summarize on your device. Its setup recommends OpenWhispr Cloud, though, and device sync, its MCP server, and team features come with paid plans. LokalBot runs on your computer by default, keeps every feature free in one GPLv3 app, and remembers more of your workday than meetings.

Verdict

Which one fits you?

Pick OpenWhispr if…

A dictation-first app with optional cloud extras.

  • Dictation is your main job: a voice assistant, translation, per-app tone, and snippets
  • You want it on an iPhone, an iPad, or an Intel Mac too
  • You'd like optional cloud sync, note sharing, and team spaces

Pick LokalBot if…

Local by default, and more than meetings.

  • You want everything on your computer unless you approve a server
  • You want AI tools to read your library through a free, local, read-only MCP server
  • You want a day timeline, screen recall, and autocomplete in the same app
  • Copyleft matters: GPLv3 keeps every fork's improvements open

Questions

The short answers.

Is OpenWhispr local?

It can be. Choose on-device transcription and a built-in local model, and your audio stays on your computer. Its setup recommends OpenWhispr Cloud, though, and transcription is chosen separately for dictation, meetings, and file uploads, so check each one. LokalBot runs on your computer by default and asks before any context goes to a remote server.

How do AI agents reach each one?

OpenWhispr's MCP server is hosted: it needs a paid plan with cloud backup turned on and sees only notes synced to its cloud. Its local CLI is free. LokalBot's read-only CLI and MCP server run on your computer, read your local library, and are free; you turn them on in Settings.

Which one runs on Linux?

Both. OpenWhispr ships AppImage, .deb, .rpm, and .tar.gz builds for x86-64 Linux. LokalBot's Linux app is in alpha, with .deb, .rpm, and Arch packages and updates through apt and dnf; see local AI meeting notes on Linux.
