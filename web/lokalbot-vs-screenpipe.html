LokalBot vs Screenpipe

Screenpipe records your screen and audio continuously and turns that history into context for any AI, through a local API and MCP server. It runs on macOS, Windows, and Linux. LokalBot starts smaller: it logs app and window activity by default, adds screen text and encrypted screenshots only if you opt in, and pairs that with bot-free meeting notes. The licenses differ too — LokalBot is GPLv3 open source, while Screenpipe's source is available under a commercial license.

Verdict

Which one fits you?

Pick Screenpipe if…

Maximum capture, built as a data layer for agents.

  • You want everything on screen and in your ears recorded, all the time
  • You're building agents and automations on top of a local API
  • You use Windows, Linux, or an Intel Mac
  • You're rolling it out to a team with managed seats

Pick LokalBot if…

Recall that starts small and asks before it captures more.

  • You'd rather log app activity by default and opt into screen text
  • You want meeting notes at the center, with summaries written locally
  • You want captures cleaned up after 14 days, not 30 GB a month
  • You want OSI-approved open source you can use at work for free

Questions

The short answers.

Isn't Screenpipe open source?

It used to be MIT-licensed. Current versions are source-available under the Screenpipe Commercial License: free for personal, non-profit, educational, and research use, while business use needs a paid license. LokalBot is GPLv3, so anyone can use it at work, study it, and share changes.

Does LokalBot record my screen all the time?

No. Out of the box it saves the app name and window title. Visible text and encrypted screenshots are separate opt-ins that need macOS Accessibility and Screen Recording permission, and captures are deleted after 14 days by default.

Can AI agents read my LokalBot history?

Yes, if you allow it. The bundled CLI and MCP server are read-only and stay off until you enable Agent Access in Settings → Privacy.
