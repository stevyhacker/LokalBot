GDPR & compliance

GDPR AI meeting notes with zero cloud subprocessors

Most 'GDPR-compliant' AI meeting tools still upload call audio to vendor servers and downstream LLM APIs, creating a subprocessor chain and Data Processing Agreements to review. Running speech recognition and summaries on your own computer removes cloud processors from the loop, while keeping participant consent squarely in focus.

What local processing solves under GDPR, and what it does not

When LokalBot runs with its built-in Apache-2.0 local models (Qwen3-ASR for transcription, Qwen3.5 4B LokalBot v4 for summaries, EmbeddingGemma 2 for search), no audio, transcript, or summary touches a LokalBot server because no LokalBot cloud backend exists. That eliminates third-party processor agreements (Article 28), cloud subprocessor audits, and international data transfers (Chapter V) for your meeting pipeline.

Local software does not waive your legal duties as the data controller:

  • Transparency and consent (Articles 6 & 13): Recording without a visible bot never excuses secret recording. Tell every participant before recording starts and follow applicable national wiretapping and workplace laws (such as Germany's Section 201 StGB or EU employment rules).
  • Data minimization and retention (Article 5): Keep only what you need. LokalBot starts day tracking activity-only, keeps screen captures off unless enabled, expires screenshots after 14 days by default, and lets you delete individual meetings or audio tracks locally.
  • Opt-in remote boundaries: If you later connect an external API, Claude/ChatGPT plan, or cloud-synced Obsidian vault, that external service becomes a processor for whatever you send to it. LokalBot requires explicit per-origin approval before sending context off the machine.

A 5-point verification checklist for DPOs and IT teams

  1. Inspect network traffic in flight: Download the open models once, disable automatic update checks, disconnect Wi-Fi (or monitor with Little Snitch / firewall logs), and run a full meeting record, transcribe, and summarize pass with zero outbound packets.
  2. Verify storage encryption: Confirm the local library sits inside your OS-encrypted disk (FileVault, BitLocker, or LUKS), with LokalBot encrypting saved screenshots and speaker voice profiles at rest via AES-GCM.
  3. Verify fresh-install consent prompts: Confirm that fresh installs ask via a local notification before recording a detected call rather than silently auto-recording every meeting.
  4. Audit the license and source: Review LokalBot's GPLv3 repository and PRIVACY.md contract directly instead of relying on marketing claims.
  5. Check language support: LokalBot's UI supports 24 languages (including German, French, Spanish, and Portuguese), and Qwen3-ASR 1.7B transcribes 52 languages locally.

FAQ

Questions about this guide

Do I need a Data Processing Agreement (DPA) to use LokalBot?

When you run LokalBot with its built-in local models, no meeting data is sent to the LokalBot project or any hosted service, so there is no LokalBot cloud processor handling your personal data. If you opt into an external API or cloud plan in Settings, you need a DPA with that provider.

Is bot-free local recording automatically legal under GDPR?

No. GDPR requires a lawful basis (often explicit consent) and clear notice to every participant before recording a call, regardless of whether a bot joins or the audio stays on your laptop.

How does LokalBot compare to European cloud notetakers like Jamie?

Jamie hosts notes on EU servers in Frankfurt and deletes raw audio after transcription, which still requires trusting cloud servers and paying EUR 24-47/mo. LokalBot processes and stores everything on your own device for free under GPLv3. See LokalBot vs Jamie.
