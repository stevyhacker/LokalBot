Best local and open-source AI meeting note takers (2026)

Six bot-free meeting note takers that transcribe on your own computer, compared on default privacy, measured local accuracy, RAM usage, MCP access, and open-source licenses.

Updated November 26, 2026 · 10 min read

Disclosure: LokalBot is our app. Every claim about the other five projects comes from their public sites, licenses, documentation, and release notes checked on November 26, 2026. Nobody paid to be listed. If a price, license, or platform detail changes, open an issue on GitHub.

The short answer

All six tools avoid sending a visible bot into your Zoom, Teams, or Google Meet calls, and all six can transcribe audio on your computer. They split cleanly by scope, license, and how much setup you want:

  • Best all-in-one local meeting library with free MCP and fine-tuned models: LokalBot (GPLv3, free, Mac plus Windows and Linux in alpha).
  • Best MIT-licensed Windows + Mac meeting recorder if you want commercial Pro support: Meetily.
  • Best bring-your-own-Ollama meeting notepad: Anarlog (formerly Hyprnote).
  • Best dictation-first cross-platform tool (including Intel Mac and standalone iOS): OpenWhispr (switch off its default cloud option during setup).
  • Best Mac file and subtitle transcriber: MacWhisper (proprietary, one-time Pro license).
  • Best continuous 24/7 screen and audio capture layer for custom agent pipes: Screenpipe (source-available, commercial license for work).

The six local meeting note takers, one by one

Each card lists what the app runs locally, where it sends data if anywhere, its exact license, and its tradeoffs.

LokalBot

Our app: bot-free meetings, fine-tuned local models, and workday memory

LokalBot is our app. It captures your microphone and meeting app audio on two synchronized tracks without joining the call, then transcribes and summarizes on your computer by default. Version 0.14.0 ships four Apache-2.0 fine-tuned defaults: Qwen3-ASR 1.7B (LokalBot v3) and 0.6B (v1) for speech, Qwen3.5 4B (LokalBot v4) for notes and Personal Context memory, and Gemma 4 E2B (LokalBot v4) for inline autocomplete. Everything sits in one local SQLite library with hybrid search (EmbeddingGemma 2), Obsidian Markdown export, opt-in screen memory, dictation, and a free read-only local MCP server.

Good for

  • Keeping audio, transcripts, summaries, and MCP access strictly on your machine with no account or paid tier.
  • Teams or solo engineers using Mac, Windows, or Linux (alpha) alongside Obsidian, Claude Code, Codex, or Cursor.
  • Bilingual calls and custom dictation vocabulary where stock small speech models drift or hallucinate on silence.

Watch out for

  • Windows and Linux apps are native Rust + GPUI alphas; the Windows installer is not code-signed yet and a few features (Nemotron diarization, Gmail briefs, Apple Intelligence) remain Mac only.
  • No Intel Mac build (requires Apple Silicon M1+ and macOS 15+, or x64 on Windows and Linux).
  • No hosted team workspace; sharing happens via Markdown export or LAN/Tailscale encrypted sync to your own iOS or Android device.

Meetily

Popular MIT community notetaker with a paid Pro edition

Meetily has one of the largest communities in open-source meeting notes. Its free Community Edition captures microphone and system audio without a bot on macOS and Windows, transcribes locally with Whisper or Parakeet, and summarizes with built-in Qwen 3.5 models, Ollama, or your own API key. Advanced features such as speaker separation, automatic meeting detection, and MCP access live in a separate commercial Pro codebase.

Good for

  • Windows and Mac users who want a mature MIT-licensed local meeting recorder with a large community.
  • Teams that want a self-hosted Enterprise option with commercial support.

Watch out for

  • Speaker separation, automatic meeting detection, CLI, webhooks, and MCP require Meetily Pro ($10/user/mo annual) on a separate commercial codebase.
  • Linux currently requires building from source; no built-in dictation, screen memory, or autocomplete.

Anarlog (formerly Hyprnote)

MIT-licensed meeting notepad with bring-your-own LLM flexibility

Anarlog (originally launched as Hyprnote, while hyprnote.com now points to the team's newer Char product) is an open-source, local-first meeting notepad. It focuses tightly on call notes and lets you plug in Ollama, LM Studio, Apple Intelligence, your own API keys, or optional hosted AI and encrypted cloud sync. It stores notes locally in SQLite and plain files and includes a local CLI and MCP server.

Good for

  • Users who already run Ollama or LM Studio and want a clean MIT-licensed notepad dedicated to meetings.
  • People who want optional encrypted cloud sync and hosted sharing alongside local files.

Watch out for

  • On-device transcription is limited to supported Macs; Linux and Windows betas rely on a cloud provider or a self-hosted server.
  • No bundled llama.cpp summary model out of the box, and no built-in dictation, day timeline, or autocomplete.

OpenWhispr

MIT dictation and bot-free meeting notes across Mac, Windows, Linux, and iOS

OpenWhispr pairs system-wide voice dictation with bot-free meeting capture under the MIT license. It ships prebuilt packages for macOS (including Intel Macs), Windows, Linux (AppImage, .deb, .rpm, archive), and iOS. During setup it recommends OpenWhispr Cloud, so privacy-focused users need to explicitly select local speech models (Orukeet or Whisper) and built-in llama.cpp or Ollama summaries.

Good for

  • Heavy dictation users who want per-app tone modes, snippets, translation, and standalone iPhone dictation.
  • Intel Mac users who still want local transcription and meeting capture.

Watch out for

  • Setup steers toward OpenWhispr Cloud by default; check local settings separately for dictation, meetings, and file uploads.
  • Its MCP server is hosted on paid plans ($8-$16/mo) and requires cloud-synced notes; only the local CLI is free.

MacWhisper

Polished Mac specialist for transcribing files and calls locally

MacWhisper is the long-standing macOS utility for turning audio and video files into transcripts and subtitles on-device. It also records Zoom, Teams, Webex, and Discord calls without a bot and offers system-wide dictation. Unlike open-source meeting libraries, MacWhisper is proprietary and focuses on file transcription first, using Ollama or cloud API keys you supply for AI summaries.

Good for

  • Journalists, podcasters, and researchers who transcribe pre-recorded audio/video files and export SRT/VTT subtitles.
  • Mac users who prefer a one-time license (Pro EUR 64) for a dedicated transcription utility.

Watch out for

  • Closed source, macOS only (plus a separate iOS app); no Windows or Linux version.
  • No built-in local meeting library search, cross-meeting Ask, MCP server, or workday memory.

Screenpipe

Continuous 24/7 local screen and audio capture built as an agent data layer

Screenpipe runs on macOS, Windows, and Linux and records your screen and audio continuously, exposing everything through a local REST API, MCP server, and programmable agent pipes. Meeting transcription with speaker labels and summary pipes sit on top of that continuous capture stream. While its code is public, current releases use the source-available Screenpipe Commercial License rather than an OSI open-source license.

Good for

  • Developers building custom local automations and agent pipes over a continuous screen and audio log.
  • Intel Mac, Windows, and Linux users who want always-on capture rather than meeting-first workflows.

Watch out for

  • Continuous capture uses roughly 30 GB of disk per month and requires a paid commercial license for business use ($21/mo Basic, $42/seat Business).
  • Does not ship a built-in fine-tuned summarization model by default; you connect your own AI provider or local runner.

What to verify before trusting a local meeting app

  1. Default network path: Download the models, turn off Wi-Fi, and run a 60-second test call. Some "local" apps still default to cloud transcription or cloud summaries during onboarding.
  2. License boundaries: Distinguish OSI open source (GPLv3, MIT, Apache-2.0) from source-available commercial licenses or split open-core codebases where speaker labels and MCP move behind a paywall.
  3. Hardware fit: On an 8 GB or 16 GB laptop, a compact speech model (0.6-1.0 GB) plus a 4B quantized summary model (~2.7 GB) leaves room for your browser and video call. Test exact residency in our Hardware Calculator.
  4. Consent still applies: Recording without a bot keeps audio off third-party servers, but legal and workplace recording rules still require telling participants before you record.

FAQ

Questions about this guide

What is the best completely free, open-source local AI meeting note taker?

LokalBot (GPLv3) ships every feature in one free app with no paid tier: bot-free two-track capture, fine-tuned Apache-2.0 local models for transcription and summaries, speaker separation, Obsidian export, and a free read-only local MCP server. Meetily Community Edition (MIT) is also free for core local recording and summaries, with speaker separation and MCP in its paid Pro tier.

Do local AI meeting note takers work completely offline?

Yes, once the speech and language models are downloaded. LokalBot, Meetily Community Edition, and MacWhisper transcribe offline. In OpenWhispr, choose on-device models during setup instead of its recommended cloud option. Anarlog transcribes locally on supported Macs and pairs with Ollama or LM Studio for offline summaries.

How much RAM do I need for local meeting transcription and summaries?

A 16 GB Apple Silicon Mac or a 16 GB Windows or Linux PC comfortably runs a 0.6B-1.7B speech model (1.0-2.5 GB) and a 4B quantized summary model (~2.7 GB) sequentially. On 8 GB machines, use compact speech models like Qwen3-ASR 0.6B or Parakeet 0.6B and smaller summarizers. Check your exact hardware at lokalbot.com/hardware.

Which local meeting note takers run on Linux and Windows?

LokalBot (native Rust + GPUI alpha with .deb, .rpm, Arch, and Windows installer), OpenWhispr (AppImage, .deb, .rpm, Windows), Screenpipe (AppImage, Windows), and Anarlog (beta; cloud or BYO transcription server on Linux/Windows). Meetily ships native Windows binaries and builds from source on Linux. MacWhisper is macOS and iOS only.

Which local meeting note taker has a free local MCP server for Claude Code or Cursor?

LokalBot includes a free, read-only local stdio MCP server (lokalbot-cli mcp, off until enabled in Settings) with no cloud sync required. Anarlog and Screenpipe also expose local MCP servers. Meetily gates MCP to Pro, and OpenWhispr's MCP server is hosted on paid plans.
