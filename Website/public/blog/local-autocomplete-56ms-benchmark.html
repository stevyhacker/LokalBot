Benchmark

56 ms local system-wide autocomplete: Gemma 4 E2B vs. LFM2.5 1.2B

System-wide inline autocomplete only feels invisible when it stays under ~100 ms per keystroke and knows when to stay silent. Here are the measured numbers behind LokalBot's Balanced and Lightweight presets.

November 17, 2026 · Updated November 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Measuring autocomplete without fooling ourselves

Our original 28-scenario autocomplete check had five flaws: it treated an intentional confidence-gate silence as a safety failure, accepted only one exact word tail for partial-word completions, mixed 10–40 s cold startup into steady-state latency, stole keyboard focus by launching the full app, and never measured next-word accuracy.

We replaced it with a headless engine runner (--cotyping-bench) that exercises the exact production LlamaCotypingRuntime with hybrid decoding, certified prefix search, and prefix healing across 28 safety/behaviour checks (5 deterministic passes) plus 96 phrases replayed at all 788 word boundaries and 8 phrases typed one character at a time.

What the numbers actually say

  • In English, the two models are effectively tied (+1.1 points, 95% bootstrap interval −1.4 to +3.5), while LFM2.5 is 2–3× faster per keystroke (26 ms median) and weighs only 0.73 GB.
  • Gemma 4 E2B earns the Balanced default on precision and non-English text. When it chooses to show a suggestion, it is right 50.1% of the time vs. 43.0% for LFM2.5, and its non-English lead (+7.3 points) comes largely from languages like Serbian where LFM2.5 stayed silent.
  • Silence is a feature. Both models withheld suggestions on the exact same 3 open-ended prompts (notes-list, comma-clause, sentence-start) and stayed quiet mid-word (rec|eive), producing zero unsafe suggestions across all 140 passes.

First suggestion after a pause (November 18, 2026)

Steady-state speed only tells half the story: after three minutes without a decode, llama.cpp releases Metal weight residency (180 s default keep-alive), and the GPU clocks down within seconds. On an M4 Max (Gemma 4 E2B Q6_K), warming the runtime with a single throwaway token on wake, unlock, click, or the first keystroke (then removing that token so the cached prompt stays intact) brings the first suggestion back to steady-state latency:

Four warm-up touches over a five-minute pause added 18 mJ of CPU energy (468 vs. 450 mJ) and never reload a model freed under memory pressure. A 30-minute pause under memory pressure—where OS file paging dominates—has not been re-measured yet. Full methodology and raw logs live in Benchmarks/autocomplete/Cotyping/results/2026-11-18-first-after-pause/REPORT.md.

Choosing between them in LokalBot

Both presets ship built into LokalBot: Balanced local uses Gemma 4 E2B Base, and Lightweight local switches to LFM2.5 1.2B Instruct for faster typing and a smaller disk footprint. For a feature-by-feature comparison with Cotypist and Cotabby, see Autocomplete that knows your meetings. Full category breakdowns and commands to rerun the engine harness are in Benchmarks/autocomplete/Cotyping/results/2026-10-15-engine-benchmark/REPORT.md.
