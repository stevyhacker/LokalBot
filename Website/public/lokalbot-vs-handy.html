LokalBot vs Handy, VoiceInk & Voicenotes

People searching for an open-source Wispr Flow replacement usually land on three distinct tools: Handy (MIT, free cross-platform Tauri push-to-talk dictation), VoiceInk (GPLv3 macOS and iOS dictation, free to compile or $25-$49 one-time for signed binaries), and Voicenotes (proprietary cloud voice-memo and meeting app at ~$99.99/yr). LokalBot sits beside Handy and VoiceInk as a free, open-source GPLv3 app with zero-hallucination local dictation, then adds bot-free two-track meeting notes, diarization, semantic search, 56 ms autocomplete, and local MCP in one memory budget.

Verdict

Which one fits you?

Pick Handy, VoiceInk, or Voicenotes if…

Each is a strong focused pick when you only want dictation or mobile voice memos.

  • Pick Handy if you want the lightest MIT-licensed dictation-only app (including Intel Macs)
  • Pick VoiceInk if you want a dedicated Mac/iOS dictation tool with custom AI rewrite modes and one-time pricing
  • Pick Voicenotes if you want cloud-synced mobile and smartwatch voice journaling

Pick LokalBot if…

Built for people who want one free local stack for both dictation and meetings.

  • You want free signed builds plus fine-tuned Qwen3-ASR v3 with zero silence hallucinations
  • You want dictation and bot-free meeting notes sharing the exact same local speech model in RAM
  • You want speaker diarization, Obsidian Markdown export, 56 ms autocomplete, and local MCP
  • You want cross-platform Mac, Windows, Linux (alpha) plus E2EE iOS/Android companion sync with no cloud subscription

Questions

The short answers.

How does LokalBot compare directly to Handy?

Handy (github.com/cjpais/Handy) is a great, minimal MIT-licensed push-to-talk dictation app that works on macOS (including Intel), Windows, and Linux. Handy stops at pasting dictated text. LokalBot adds bot-free two-track meeting recording, speaker diarization, local summaries, semantic search, Obsidian export, autocomplete, and a read-only MCP server.

Why use LokalBot instead of VoiceInk on Mac?

Both are GPLv3 and run local speech models on Apple Silicon. VoiceInk sells signed binaries ($25-$49) and focuses deeply on dictation modes and prompt rewrites. LokalBot ships free signed and notarized Mac builds, fine-tuned Qwen3-ASR models that eliminate silence prompt echoes, and full meeting memory and coding-agent MCP.

Can LokalBot replace Voicenotes for quick thoughts on a phone?

LokalBot's free iOS and Android companion apps record audio offline and sync end-to-end encrypted over LAN or Tailscale to your computer for local transcription and search. Voicenotes is better if you want standalone cloud transcription on a smartwatch without a home computer.
