# Day tracking — Today, Timeline, and Ask

Part of the [development guide](../../DEVELOPMENT.md).

- **Default modes:** fresh settings select activity-only day tracking. Accessible text and encrypted visual context require opt-in and applicable macOS grants. Settings → Screen & Activity → **Track app & window activity** can also turn collection fully off.
- **Sampler:** frontmost app + focused-window title every 5 s, idle-aware (3 min), minimum 5 s block, pause/resume from the menu bar. A metadata-only Accessibility read checks secure fields and excluded apps/domains without reading document text; browsers, web-based apps, and private windows are tracked by name and title. Only excluded apps and excluded sites become anonymous “Private” duration blocks; other samples keep the app name, and the title unless a secure field is focused or a site exclusion cannot be ruled out. Stored in `activity_blocks` (same SQLite db).
- **Timeline sessions:** titles use duration-ranked observed window/document titles, with the original app/time blocks available for inspection. Sessions split at gaps over five minutes, a sustained new document after ten minutes, or the next source boundary after 45 minutes. Individual and overlapping blocks stay intact; brief app interruptions do not force a new session. These labels describe captured evidence, not inferred tasks.
- **Today:** the default landing page combines capture state, the current day digest, open meeting actions, upcoming-meeting preparation, and direct routes to Ask, Timeline, and Agent Mode. The reading column puts decisions and next steps before the current work summary. The shared digest keeps freshness in a compact footer and the expandable Yesterday recap below current work; previous-day provenance is inside that disclosure. Calendar setup lives in Settings. The next meeting leads with Join/Record, and the rest of today's meetings follow in a compact Later today list. Events with other attendees or a conferencing link are meetings; a solo event is listed without Join/Record when its title names a meeting (for example “Product Standup”), and personal appointments are left out. Once a meeting summary has completed (`ProcessingPipeline.onSummaryCompleted`) or LokalBot has been used on seven distinct days (it became the active app or showed Today), the page ends with a one-time request to star the GitHub repository (`GitHubStarPrompt`); either button retires it, and Settings → General → About and the menu-bar menu keep a permanent **Star on GitHub** link. UI tests, captures, unit tests, headless runs, and libraries planted by `Scripts/test/seed_demo_library.py` never show the request or count toward it.
- **Timeline:** one day as its page beside a rail. The date is the window's title (`TimelineContentView` in `CaptureView.swift`): it opens a calendar, with previous/next arrows, a Today button on other days, and **Ask about this day** beside it. One … menu writes, updates or rewrites the digest, copies or exports it, pauses or resumes day memory, and reloads the day; while day memory is paused the rail says so with **Resume**. The rail (`TimelineRail`) is the trailing pane of a native split, so its divider can be dragged between 260 and 540 pt and the split remembers the width (`LokalBot.timeline.rail`). Until it is dragged, its width follows the window and the text size (`TimelineRailMetric`: 352 pt in a roomy window, 300 pt in the default 1,180 pt one, giving way down to 260 pt before the page gets tighter than 420 pt). From the top it holds the tracked total with the meetings and what was produced (`TimelineTrackedSummary`), one day strip (`TimelineDayStrip`: the hours left to right, meetings in a lane above work, coloured by project or by app on a day without projects, each bar selecting its row; a thin bar is clicked over a wider area than it is drawn) with a legend of each project's time as rows, Meetings last; then **Sessions** with its count, the day's work sessions and meetings in time order (built by `TimelineDayList`) under Morning (before 12:00), Afternoon (before 17:00) and Evening, with a stretch of 30 minutes or more without activity kept as "Away 58m"; and **All captured activity** pinned as its bottom row. A row has two lines: the title, then project and what was produced for work ("Atlas · 1 PR opened", from `TimelineWorkSession.projectLabel`/`taskTitle` and its agent outcomes) or "Meeting · app" for a meeting, with the duration at the right. A session spent in the app a recorded meeting ran in folds into that meeting when the meeting covers at least 60% of it, so a call is listed once; work with a project or coding-agent sessions is never folded, and the folded activity stays under All captured activity. The main area shows the day's page (`TimelineDigestPage`) until a row is chosen, as panels (`SectionPanel`) that fill it inside 24 pt gutters, up to 1,100 pt: **Highlights**, whose footer says when the digest was written, with **Update** when there is newer activity; **Actions from this day's meetings**, the shared `ActionThreadRow` rows with an "N open" badge and a link to Actions, or the header alone with "All done" (`TimelineDayActions`); **Work Summary**, with Blocked and In progress, project tags in the sessions' colours and their next step, and Done folded into one line, or an older digest's plain entries; and **Decisions** when the day has them. A day without a digest shows an invitation with **Write Digest** in the Highlights panel; a stale digest stays visible, marked. Choosing a row, or a bar of the strip, shows it in the main area while the rail stays: a session's title panel (project, time range, active time and apps), then panels for what the coding agent was asked and recorded doing, its screen moments as thumbnails with **Show all**, and what was open (each title opens the activity behind it); a meeting's short preview (recap, your actions, decisions, **Open meeting**). **All captured activity** keeps the per-block hour track for exact evidence and **Delete screen moments…**, the reviewed time-range deletion of screen captures. **Day digest** above such a page returns to the day, as does choosing the selected row again; an activity block or a screen moment opened from a page has a link back to it. Up and Down in the rail walk the sessions: Down from the day's page chooses the first one, Up from the first returns to the day's page, and Down from the last chooses All captured activity (`TimelineListSelection`). The body keeps the page (420 pt minimum) beside the rail (260 pt at its narrowest), so below that the window stops shrinking instead of switching to a drawer.
- **People:** `PeopleDirectory` joins calendar attendee names, speaker names the user applied (and their calendar-identity links), and action owners into one person per attendee address (used only as a private join key, never shown or exported), or per full name. An attendee without a display name takes the rename picker's address-derived name, except single-word mailboxes on public providers, which are usually handles. A lone first name joins the one person whose full name starts with it. Speaker labels, group or role owners ("Engineering team", "Product/research"), owner lists ("Matt, John, and Emil"), merge suffixes, and the Mac account's name are never people. Corrections the user makes on the page (`PeopleStore`, `people/curation.json`, keyed by opaque person ids) apply on every build: a chosen name is shown and also recognized as a name, merges run after the automatic joins and again once action owners are known (the target keeps its name, and **Separate** undoes one), and **Not a person** removes the entry from People, Projects, and the CLI. Recordings of one calendar event on one day, or without an event the same title starting within 15 minutes of the previous recording's end, count as one meeting that opens its longest recording; meetings are grouped by title. Each person lists what is waiting on the user (open actions naming them or from meetings with at most two other people) and on them, with **Still open?** on open work no meeting has mentioned for 21 days and that is not due ahead; **Last time** (or **Last mentioned**), up to three summary lines from the newest of their last eight meetings that the person said (an applied speaker name) or that name them with a capital letter (a name that is also a common word does not count at a sentence start), skipping action and decision sections, or a meeting's overview lines when it was only with them; projects whose names appear in those lines, their open work, and decisions with them; decisions from meetings of at most four people, or in larger meetings only those they said or that name them; and meetings together by title. Decisions of one meeting keep one order between rebuilds. The page (`PeopleWorkspaceView`, `PersonDetailView`) shows only sections that have something in them: the next calendar meeting with the person as a card with **Prepare** (the same Agent prompt as the menu's **Prepare in Agent…**, built by `PersonPreparation` from the person's notes, projects, open work and decisions, with nothing from the calendar event), **Open between you** as one section with an amber "You owe <name>" label and a grey "<name> owes you" label over the shared `ActionThreadRow`, **Last time**, and **Decided together** (three, then **Show N more**). Projects, Meetings (titles with their count and last date; a series opens to list its dates) and Names sit in a side column that moves under the page when the pane is narrower than `PageSideColumnLayout` allows; the toolbar holds the person's name, how long you have been meeting, **Ask about <name>** and the … menu (Prepare, Draft follow-up, Rename, Merge into, Separate, Not a person). The list (`PeopleListContent`) is grouped by time only: **Coming up** (people in calendar meetings in the next seven days, by meeting time), **Last 2 weeks**, **Earlier**, each by when you last met; a row shows the next or latest meeting and one badge, "You owe N" (amber) or "Owes you N", the user's own first when both are open. `PeopleUpcomingMeetings` matches the coming week's events (`UpcomingMeetingSchedule.readAhead`, read only while Calendar use is on and access is granted, never requested from People, refreshed each minute while the page is open) to people by the keys the directory registers: an attendee's address, else the full name of someone known only by name, and for an attendee without an address the one person carrying that full name or whose full name starts with a lone first name; two people who share a name are never guessed between, an event some recording already covers is left out, and the match is kept in memory only. People never reads voice profiles. `WorkspacePageBlocks.swift` holds the list-and-page pieces People and Projects share (`ListColumnSearchField`, `PageSectionHeading`, `PageDecisionRow`, `PageWithSideColumn`, `SideColumnBlock`, `LabelChip`). Actions can be filtered by person. `WorkMemoryConnections` rebuilds People and Projects off the main actor, reading transcripts for applied names only from the last 180 days, and whole summaries and `transcript.md` for Projects and person notes from the last 120 days, each cached by file date.
- **Projects:** the person tracks projects (`ProjectStore`, `projects/tracked.json`: a name, other names, and hidden suggestions); everything else is derived. `ProjectLinks` joins each tracked project to meetings from the last 120 days whose title, whole summary, outcomes, or transcript contain every significant word of one of its names; actions that name it or come from a meeting whose title names it; decisions naming it; overnight review projects whose names it covers; people from its meetings; and the last seven days of window time whose titles name it, per day, with the six longest windows and a count of all of them. Actions are split into the user's, other people's mentioned in the last 14 days or due ahead, and older ones. Each meeting carries the first summary line naming the project, else a transcript line with its time, else the action or decision, cut to 180 characters around the name. Suggestions are names `ProjectTopicDetector` finds and overnight review projects no tracked project covers. The detector keeps proper nouns that are nearly always capitalized and appear in at least three meetings' titles, summaries, actions, and decisions, excluding people's names and short forms, app names, and generic work vocabulary; a capitalized sentence start ("**Them 4:** Vireo…") counts only for a term also seen capitalized mid-sentence. **Where it stands** (`ProjectStatusGenerator`) asks the Think model for an overview, up to five points citing their meeting, and open questions from the ten newest meetings' lines, decisions and open actions, and review notes; notes are saved in `projects/summaries.json` with a hash of that evidence, kept and marked "Newer evidence since this was written." when it changes, and written automatically on open only under `allowsAutomaticMainInference` with a ready model, once per evidence version. Deleting a meeting (`withPrimaryEvidenceChange(.removal)`) withdraws every note that used it. The list (`ProjectsListContent`) has a search field and the add button as its header, then **Tracked**, most recently active first, each with "Active today", "Active yesterday", "Last active <day>", "Quiet since <date>" once nothing has named it for `quietAfterDays` (the same 14 days as `ProjectLinks.recentOthersActionDays`; such rows are drawn fainter) or "Nothing linked yet", and the count of the user's open actions; then **Suggested**, with a dashed monogram and "In N meetings" or "From overnight review". Hidden suggestions come back from the link at its foot. A tracked project's page (`ProjectDetailView`) leads with the **Where it stands** card, the only model-written block: a sparkle, one line from the saved note saying when it was written, by which model, and on this Mac or which server (`ProjectsWording.provenance`), and **Update** (or **Write note**); while a new note is written the old one stays, dimmed. Facts follow outside the card: **Your actions** and **Waiting on others** (three, then **Show N more**, recent before older) on the shared `ActionThreadRow`, **Decisions** with their meeting, and **Meetings** (four, then **Show all N**), each with the line that names the project and a ▶ time that plays a transcript line. Overnight review notes are evidence for the note and are not listed on a tracked project's page. A side column (`PageWithSideColumn`, as in People) holds **Last 7 days** (the total, a bar per day from `ProjectTimePresentation.days` with zero for a day without time, the three longest windows and "N more windows"), **People**, and **Names** with **Edit names…**. A suggestion's page opens on the decision in one card: where it came up (the overnight review's own words carry the sparkle), then **Add to <project>** first when that tracked project already has at least half of the suggestion's meetings (`ProjectSuggestionHome`), with **Track as its own project** and **Not a project**; otherwise **Track** first, with **Add to a project** and **Not a project**. Tracking opens the new project, adding opens the project it joined, and **Not a project** opens the next suggestion. A tracked project nothing names yet is one centred message with **Add other names…**, which opens the names sheet. The toolbar holds the project's name, its last activity (or "Tracked since <date>", or "Suggested"), **Ask about <project>** and, for a tracked project, the … menu (Edit names…, Merge into, Stop tracking).
- **Morning brief actions:** Dream action candidates carry `[A1]`-style handles; the model is asked to begin a top action with its handle, and `DreamPrompts.linkTopActions` maps handles to thread ids (`DreamReport.topActionThreadIDs`), so linked actions render as checkable threads. The evidence-only fallback links its ranked threads too.
- **Day digest:** "Write digest" / "Update digest" runs the configured LLM over the day's blocks, meetings, and captured text → `journal/YYYY-MM-DD.md`. The same digest is visible from Today and Timeline, can be copied or exported, and grounds **Ask about day**. Each evidence segment is extracted separately: substantive work leads the task list, and lighter but identifiable work follows it instead of being dropped. A segment, about an hour of continuous activity, returns its main work item plus up to two other distinct items (`other_tasks`), so an hour that interleaves a fix, a review, and an email need not pack them into one line. On the synthetic benchmark in `Benchmarks/DayDigest/` this raised items with their own task line from 34% to 62% with Qwen3.5 4B. Work done through an AI assistant or coding agent counts as substantive when the evidence names a concrete task. External Think servers share one request gate per origin (`RemoteInferenceGate`): processing runs at most two requests at once, scheduled work (digest, Dream, briefs) on at most one of them, meeting notes at `pipeline` priority ahead of it, chat, dictation, and agents never queue behind processing, and a 429 pauses the whole origin for `Retry-After` or an exponential cooldown (2–60 s). The gated engine retries transient errors (429, 5xx, dropped connection) up to five attempts for notes, four for scheduled work, and two for chat, so callers do not replay on top. Segments are sized by active time: one per hour of a session (never more than it has events), at most 16 a day, instead of a fixed eight. Answers to unchanged segment and aggregation prompts are reused from an in-memory `DayDigestSegmentCache`, purged for a day whenever its evidence is deleted or corrected. After an evidence change the scheduler waits 20 s for notes, outcomes, and indexing to settle, and keeps a failure's 15-minute backoff.
- **Coding agent sessions:** on by default, chosen in setup's first step and in Settings → Screen & Activity. A new install reads nothing until setup is finished: the ingestor's configuration has no agents until `AppState.onboardingShownKey` is set, and `AppState.finishOnboarding` starts it. While enabled, `CodingAgentEvidenceIngestor` reads Claude Code and Codex transcripts (`LokalBot/Services/CodingAgents/`) for the digest's seven-day window every ten minutes and before each digest run. It splits sessions into bursts at ten-minute idle gaps and stores only settled bursts in `coding_agent_bursts`, so a digest's inputs cannot change while it is written. Removing or correcting a stored burst, for example after a folder exclusion or a deleted session, goes through `withPrimaryEvidenceChange` as a revision; only **Delete saved agent sessions** withdraws the generated journal. An unreadable transcript never counts as a deletion. Bursts follow screen-text retention. The journal lists them under `## Agent sessions`, using recorded facts only: requests, actions, PRs, and changed files. The model reads each burst as `WORK SOURCE: AGENT SESSION` evidence. A segment merges a session's bursts into one detail and reserves up to six of its twelve detail slots for sessions, those with recorded outcomes first. While a session covers the time, screen text from agent host apps (Claude, Codex, terminals, editors) keeps one sample. Segment extraction yields one task per segment, which would drop parallel sessions. So each session that recorded an outcome (except scheduled runs) also becomes a task stated from its actions. These tasks are added after aggregation, merging into a model task only when the titles match. Scheduled runs' reports are withheld from the model because they often describe other days. Bursts also fill the fallback task list. Days without bursts keep their previous evidence signatures.
- **Scheduled digest and Dream:** automatic digests preview today after the configured hour and finalize every missed day of the last seven, oldest first, once its journal predates that day's end; days without evidence are skipped. Overnight review catches up the same seven-day window after its hour once LokalBot is not recording or processing and the Mac is on AC power. Both use the Think connection's origin approval (or the selected plan's approval); there is no separate scheduled-run approval.
- **Background work card:** `BackgroundActivityMonitor` combines published state from the processing pipeline, day digest runs (segment progress), the Dream scheduler, model downloads, and the semantic backfill into the sticky sidebar card above the storage summary. It shows the highest-priority activity, lists the rest in a popover, and routes each row to its meeting, day, Today, or Models settings.
- **Digest provenance:** version-2 metadata fingerprints the exact digest inputs and generated journal bytes. Dream, exports, and routines reuse a digest only while both match, including after corrections or deletion of the last source. Legacy unsigned journals remain readable but are not reused as verified evidence until regenerated. Automatic repair preserves edited signed journals; explicit meeting/screen deletions invalidate dependent day products. Only those deletions (`AppState.EvidenceChange.removal`) withdraw a generated journal. Reprocessing, transcript and outcome edits, saved moments, and re-read agent sessions are revisions: the journal stays, shown as out of date, until the scheduler or the person regenerates it. Before 2026-10-07 every revision deleted it, so a summary rerun right after a manual digest made the digest vanish. Days older than the retention window are never shown as out of date for a fingerprint mismatch alone, because expiry, not new work, explains it.
- **Refresh ordering:** generation rechecks the journal revision before saving, preserving edits made while the model runs. Persisting a digest reopens exports and routines that may have finished earlier. Meeting edits invalidate every Dream report whose 14-day comparison window includes the source. Screen, activity, coding-agent, and past-day digest changes are activity-only: they revoke the reports and memory that read that day's screens or digest and leave meeting-derived facts alone; today's digest refreshes revoke nothing. Scheduled retention is not such a change: expiring screen text, titles, and agent sessions (`AppState.withScheduledRetention`) leave that day's generated journal, Dream reports, and work memory, which outlive the screen window. The journal is not reconsidered either, so it is never rebuilt from what retention left. Settings → Overnight review → **Clear work memory** deletes all of them; a dream that started before the clear cannot write back (`DreamStore.clearAll`). An in-flight generation is checked against its own sources when it commits, so an unrelated change no longer discards it. Dream's 24,000-character evidence gives the day's journal at most 12,000: its summary, meetings, agent sessions, and time allocation first, then as much of the raw activity log as fits, so app totals, saved moments, the comparison window, and action handles always arrive. Dream requests cap output at 4,096 tokens and reasoning at 1,024; an automatic run that hits a rate limit or dropped connection is retried after the 15-minute backoff, and only the third consecutive failure for a day writes the evidence-only brief. The retention pass records its last run in the library (`.screen-retention-last-pass`), so relaunching does not repeat it.
- **Dream retraction:** app-owned provenance follows facts across memory merges. Source deletion/correction retracts dependent projects, goals, patterns, and reports, including pins; unattributed legacy facts are conservatively removed on evidence mutation. Stable meeting IDs and conservative civil-date coverage handle timezone changes. A root-level revocation intent and process-shared lock protect source writes and prevent stale in-flight commits or restart recovery from reviving withdrawn facts. Automatic screen retention uses the same boundary and deletes only reviewed rows.
