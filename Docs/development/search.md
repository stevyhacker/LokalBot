# Search & player

Part of the [development guide](../../DEVELOPMENT.md).

- **Index:** SQLite + FTS5 (`lokalbotv3.sqlite` — system SQLite, no dependency) over titles, transcript segments, summaries, the user's `notes.md`, current outcomes (decisions, actions with saved text/owner/due corrections, and open questions), and OCR'd screen text. Ask's result types include Notes and Outcomes; a one-time `search-notes-outcomes-v1` migration reindexes older libraries, and note or outcome edits enqueue a reindex. Notes are also embedded for search by meaning. Segment rows carry their audio timestamp; incremental re-index by file mtime on launch and after each pipeline run. The `unicode61` tokenizer indexes an unbroken run of Chinese, Japanese, or Korean text as one token, so FTS5 alone matched a CJK word only at the start of a phrase. `CJKSearch` (in `CLISupport/`, shared with `lokalbot-cli`) therefore matches queries containing CJK text by `LIKE` substring on the stored text, in meeting search, the Meetings filter, screen search, and the MCP screen reader. Relaxed (OR) searches split a spaceless question into at most eight two-character pieces and rank by how many matched. A query without CJK text that FTS finds nowhere falls back to the same substring match, but only in rows containing CJK text, so a Latin word glued to Chinese ("比如logo再大") is found and English rows never match by substring.
- **Semantic search:** transcript/summary chunks and retained screen OCR are embedded with EmbeddingGemma 2 GGUF (`Q8_0`, approximately 0.31 GB, 768-dimensional vectors) on a second llama-server instance (port 17873, `--embeddings -b 2048 -ub 2048`). The GGUF carries the model's mean pooling and projection, so the server sets no pooling flag; as a bidirectional encoder it needs each input in one micro-batch, hence the 2,048-token batch. Queries use the checkpoint's `task: search result | query: ` prompt and documents `title: none | text: `, with prompt caching off. Vectors live in SQLite with an index-version marker covering the model, pooling, prompts, and chunking. Queries use brute-force cosine similarity. Screen OCR is embedded only after a library first searches screens by meaning: that search starts a backfill, later captures schedule one batched pass 15 minutes on, launch resumes rows left behind, and a failed pass retries after 1 minute, doubling up to 30 minutes, until it succeeds. Screen results rank the way DigUp ranks files (`ScreenSearchRanking`, shared by Ask, Quick Recall and the assistant's `search_screen` through `ScreenSearch`): every screen vector, text and picture, is scored against the query, centred per kind (text scores higher than pictures for the same topic) and put on one z scale over the whole library, so a date or app filter never moves the scale. A capture qualifies by standing out (z ≥ 2) or by holding at least half of the query's words; its score is the z plus a word bonus that grows with that share, and lookups made only of codes weigh words double. This replaced reciprocal-rank fusion, which gave the OR-fallback keyword list the same weight as meaning and pulled the right screen down on descriptive queries ([13 October report](../../Benchmarks/search/EmbeddingGemma2/2026-10-13-screen-images/REPORT.md)). With no screen vectors yet the keyword results stay as they are. With Text + visual context, `ScreenImageEmbeddings` gives each retained screenshot a picture vector: the pixels are decrypted in memory, sized for a 280-token budget (`FileImageSizing`) and embedded on the Files media embedder (17876), newest first, in batched passes like the text backfill (15 minutes after new captures, at launch, with backoff), paused while a meeting records, in Low Power Mode or when hot, and only once screens were searched by meaning, which is also when the projector downloads. Passes are paced like Files' (`IndexPacing`), and on battery a pass stops after the newest 48 captures (`batteryLimit`), so the first pass over a library waits for a charger. A picture vector is deleted whenever its pixels are: `ActivityStore.clearScreenshotPath` (retention) deletes it before clearing the path, and deleting a capture deletes it with the row.
- **Files** (Settings → Files, `LokalBot/Services/FileSearch/`): the folders a person chooses are searched by what is in them with DigUp's engine. `DigUpKit` ([ARahim3/DigUp](https://github.com/ARahim3/DigUp), MIT; pinned by revision through the [stevyhacker/DigUp](https://github.com/stevyhacker/DigUp) fork in `project.yml`) crawls and reads them and keeps the index in `files-index.noindex` in the library; LokalBot uses none of DigUp's UI, llama.cpp build or helper processes. `FileMediaEmbedder` implements DigUp's synchronous `Embedder` over HTTP to the `media-embedder` llama-server role (17876): the text GGUF plus `mmproj-embeddinggemma-2-Q8_0.gguf` (555 MB, pinned like the text model and downloaded only when Files is on), started with `--image-min-tokens 1 --image-max-tokens 1120`. Images are resized for their token budget in the app (`FileImageSizing`, a port of DigUp's `ImageSizing` that matches Pillow bit for bit) and sent as PNG content parts; audio goes as `input_audio` WAV parts. That path reproduces DigUp's reference vectors ([report](../../Benchmarks/search/EmbeddingGemma2/2026-10-30-media-embedder/REPORT.md)), and the text-only embedder makes the same query vectors, so searches never load the projector. A server that cannot answer throws `WorkerError`, so DigUp stops the pass and keeps files pending; any other error would mark the batch failed for good. DigUp's text recognition (the words in screenshots and scanned PDF pages, and the speed check) runs on `TextRecognitionQueue` like the app's other Vision text requests: `FileTextRecognition` sets DigUpKit's `TextRecognition.reader`, and a request waits at most 20 s. With no answer the file stays pending, or a long PDF keeps its place, the pass goes on with the next file, and a later pass reads it (`testAPassFinishesWithoutTextRecognitionThatNeverAnswers`); before, one request that never returned held the pass and its model until the app quit. `FileSearchService` runs passes at launch, when the folders change, and five seconds after FSEvents report a change. Each pass syncs first and starts the media embedder only when files are new or changed, stops at the next file while a meeting records, in Low Power Mode or at a serious thermal state, and releases the server two minutes after the pass. Big reads are paced: while the person is at the Mac (input in the last two minutes, from `CGEventSource`), `FileIndexPacer` rests between DigUp's jobs for twice as long as each job took (at most 20 s), so indexing gets about a third of the time; with no input it runs at full speed. The next job is decoded ahead during the rest. On battery, unless **Read big folders on battery** (`fileSearchReadsOnBattery`, off by default) is on, a pass reads only the files its sync just found, if they are 50 or fewer (`newFilesLimit`, as DigUp's helper does), and leaves the rest of a folder's first pass and of long files for a charger (`State.waitingForPower`); IOKit's limited-power notification (`.powerSourceDidChange`) resumes it. Until a folder is chosen, Settings offers Documents, Desktop and Downloads as one-click choices (`FileSearchService.suggestedFolders`), with symbols and catalog names rather than Finder's icon and display name, which would read inside those TCC-protected folders and prompt before the person picks one; picking one opens the same sheet, and macOS asks for access when the count opens it (a declined folder is reported as `unreadable`). Before a folder is added, `FileFolderAddSheet` shows what it holds, how long its first read takes on this Mac, how much room its index will take, and any model download still to come: DigUp's `Crawler` and `Estimator` read metadata only, with the costs indexing has measured here (`costs.json` in the index folder) or DigUp's M4 Pro costs scaled by its one-second speed check, run once. The index size uses per-item bytes measured on a real DigUp index (`FileIndexSize`: about 2.7 KB a picture, 6.7 KB a screenshot, 7.7 KB a PDF page, 8.7 KB per 2 KB of document, 2 KB per 25 s of sound or video frame); `FileIndexSizeTests` indexes a mixed folder and holds the projection within 20% of what DigUp writes (it came within 1% on 31 October). Indexing and search use separate connections on their own serial queues (WAL). Recordings and video are read too (DigUp's 30-second sound windows every 25 s, and a frame per shot at most every 3 s) unless **Include recordings and video** is off, which excludes them by extension; a moment opens in an `AVPlayerView` window at its time, and for the assistant `FileMomentTranscriber` exports that 30-second window to M4A and transcribes it with the configured engine, only when `ModelReadinessSnapshot` says the model is on disk and no meeting is recording, keeping the text in memory. Code is excluded because `includeCode` is off. Ask, Quick Recall and the Ask agent's `search_files` tool (cited as `[file:ID]`, the passage id) read it when the Files source is selected. External agents search it by words through `lokalbot-cli search-files` and the MCP `search_files` tool, only under the separate Files read grant (see [Agent CLI & MCP](agent-cli.md)). Turning Files off deletes the index.
- **Agent notes** (Settings → Screen & Activity → Read coding agent notes, `LokalBot/Services/AgentMemory/`): off by default. `ClaudeCodeMemoryReader` reads `~/.claude/projects/*/memory/*.md` (front matter for the description and type, `MEMORY.md` only for titles, and the opening of one session file per project for the folder's real path); `CodexMemoryReader` reads `~/.codex/memories` (`MEMORY.md` split at `# Task Group`, `memory_summary.md` split at its sections, and `rollout_summaries/*.md`). `AgentMemoryExclusions` applies "Never read sessions in these folders" before a note leaves a reader, and every note's text passes `ScreenContextPrivacy.redact`. `AgentMemoryIngestor` scans off the main actor at launch, on a setting change and every ten minutes, and `AgentMemoryStore` makes `agent-notes.sqlite` in the library (a `notes` table plus an FTS5 `notes_fts`) match the scan; turning the setting off deletes the file. `AgentNotesIndex` (`CLISupport/AgentNotesAccess.swift`, shared with the CLI) is the query-only read side: words by BM25 with title and summary weighted, every word first and any word as a fallback, a prefix match on the last word, and a substring path for CJK. The notes are a search source only (`AskSourceScope.agentNotes`, `AskFacet.agentNotes`, the Ask tool `search_agent_notes`, `[note:ID]` citations, a Quick Recall section); they never feed the day digest, Dream, or Personal Context. There are no vectors yet, so search is by words. `AgentMemoryReaderTests` and `AgentNotesTests` cover it. The Windows and Linux app has the same source: the readers and the ingestor thread are in `LinuxWindows/crates/lokalbot-engine/src/agent_notes/`, the store, the read-only index and the grant in `crates/lokalbot-core/src/agent_notes/` (the same file and schema, so either app reads what the other wrote), and `search-notes` / `search_agent_notes` in `crates/lokalbot-cli/src/notes.rs`. There the setting is under Settings → Day Memory and the grant is its own switch, **Allow external agents to search agent notes**, in Settings → Privacy & Data.
- **UI:** sidebar Today | Actions | Meetings | Timeline | People | Projects | Ask | Agent, with Settings in its foot (it opens the Settings window). Ask is one field (`AskView`, routing in `AskRouter`/`AskPhase`): typing lists matches (`AskMatchesList`), under the field on a new question and in a tray above the docked field inside a conversation, so the conversation stays on screen. **Return asks**, whatever was typed; **Down Arrow** moves onto the matches (`AskMatchCursor`) and Return then opens the selected one with playback paused, without starting inference; Escape returns to the field and then folds the tray. The button always says Ask, is disabled while the field is empty, and becomes Stop while an answer is written. Quick Recall keeps Return for opening a result (see **Quick Recall** below). What the answer reads depends on what was typed (`AskAnswerScope`, using `AskIntent`). A full question (two or more words ending in a question mark, three or more words starting with an English question or request word such as what, who, how, did, summarize, list, or any four or more words, so a question in another language without a question mark counts: `AskAnswerScope.isFullQuestion`) reads everything in the sources and dates chosen in the composer's two menus, whether or not matches are listed; the listed matches never narrow it, and the sources menu keeps showing the chosen sources. A keyword or short phrase is a search the person then asks about: its answer is bounded to the listed meetings and screen moments, including folded lines and attached screens (`RecallWorkspaceState.selectResultEvidence`); a selected Activity source stays available, since activity has no result rows to bound, and so do Files and Agent notes when files or notes matched, and the sources menu then names that limit. With no matches, or for a "Remember …" statement, nothing is bounded. Scope set on purpose applies to both kinds and shows in the sources menu until it is widened there: a meeting or screen moments another page handed over ("Ask about this meeting"), an attached screen, and the menu's "Only …" state left by an earlier keyword. Because a full question is no longer narrowed by its matches, it is an unscoped question whenever the menus say All sources and Any time, so it gets work memory and learned Personal Context items like any other unscoped question (`ChatViewModel.run`). Scope lives only in the composer's two menus. The sources menu (`AskScopeSummary`) toggles Meetings, Activity, Screen, Files and, while Read coding agent notes is on, Agent notes, holds the match-type and screen-app filters as submenus (they constrain the listed matches only), and links to source permissions; its button names the active filters and, when a question is limited to earlier matches or to evidence another page handed over, what it is limited to, with a row that widens it again. The time menu offers Any time, Today, Yesterday, Last 7 days, and a specific date across meeting, activity, and screen search and answers. Empty results offer Clear search filters. Civil-date ranges persist with questions and survive reopening, retries and Ask again without widening retrieval. Keyword and available semantic rankings blend automatically, respecting the semantic-indexing preference. Reading excerpts remove generated metadata and malformed repetition; semantic meeting hits recover the original indexed passage instead of displaying an old truncated embedding prefix. Keyword matches appear first and semantic ones carry a **Related by meaning** mark. An empty page suggests questions built from the latest own meeting, a person and a project already loaded (`AskSuggestions`), plus one word to search; with none of those it shows the general suggestions. In an answer a citation number is a pill that opens the quoted passage in a popover (`CitationPassagePopover`: the transcript line at the citation's second, a screen moment's stored text, a file's excerpt, or a saved agent note's summary, with Play from there or Show in Finder and a link to open the source), and one sources list ends the answer (`AnswerSourcesList`, grouped by `AnswerSources`; numbers are never renumbered) with a row for what the person told LokalBot when that was used. A failed answer shows a short notice with Model settings and Retry and lists the question's matches again, searched on the Mac within the scope recorded with the question. A line under the field appears only for a remote model or a blocked model connection.
- **Quick Recall** (`QuickRecallView`, the `quick-recall` window): one small window for search, commands and Ask. The system-wide shortcut (Settings → Ask → Quick Recall, off by default), ⌘K and View → Quick Recall… all open it through `AppState.openQuickRecall`; the separate ⌘K command palette window is gone and `PaletteCommandCatalog` feeds this window. Before anything is typed it lists Recent (recent meetings and saved screen moments merged by time, `QuickRecallRecent`) and every command with its shortcut (`PaletteCommandFilter.idleOrder`: recording and dictation first). Commands that act on the main window's current page (the Agent screen's, and its tasks) are offered only when the window was opened from the main window (`quickRecallOpenedFromMainWindow`). Typing searches with `RecallSearch.search`, as before, and groups the rows in a fixed order (`QuickRecallGrouping`): commands whose words start with the typed words (`PaletteCommandFilter.split`), Meetings, Screens, Files, Agent notes while Read coding agent notes is on, and last the commands that only hold the typed words somewhere (so everything the palette found is still found, but Return on a search word never runs a command that merely contains it). Each kind lists three rows; its heading's **Show all N** opens Ask on the search with the sources narrowed to that kind (`AskNavigationHandoff.sources`), and for commands lists them all in place. The search lists at most `RecallSearch.displayLimit` of a kind, so a kind that filled it while the result says more matched reads "40+", never an exact total. A row (`QuickRecallRowModel`, `QuickRecallRow`) is two lines: a tile (a screen moment's thumbnail with a badge when it is saved, a waveform, the file's own icon, a command's symbol), the title, the app or folder in grey and when (`QuickRecallDateLabel`), then the matching line starting with who said it (`SearchIndex.Hit.speaker`), or with Your notes, Summary or Outcome, or a PDF's page. **Return opens the selected row; ⌘Return asks**, and so does Return once the arrow keys are on the Ask row pinned under the results (`QuickRecallCursor`; with nothing to open, Return does nothing). The field is an AppKit text field (`QuickRecallTextField`) that owns both typing and the list's keys, as the palette's field did, so a typed character cannot arrive twice; it takes focus without activating the app or ordering a window. Asking answers in the window: a new conversation in the shared `ChatViewModel`, shown by `ChatTranscriptView(style: .compact)` with sources as numbered rows (`QuickRecallAnswerSources`, quotes marked by `QuickRecallQuote`), a back arrow to the results, the field as "Ask a follow-up", and a foot that says where it was answered and what it read, with Copy and **Continue in main window** (`continueQuickRecallConversationInAsk`, which also sets the Ask screen's sources menu to the question's scope). What the answer reads is `QuickRecallAskScope`: a word or a phrase of up to three words is limited to the listed meetings and screen moments (all the search listed, not only the three shown), exactly as ⌘Return's handoff was; a full question (`AskAnswerScope.isFullQuestion`), a "Remember …" statement, or a search that listed no meeting or screen moment reads every source. A word asked while its matches are still being found waits for them. The Ask row names a remote model before anything is sent; remote-inference consent and the About-you gate are `ChatViewModel`'s, unchanged. `QuickRecallResultsTests`, `QuickRecallRowModelTests`, `PaletteCommandFilterTests` and `FocusOnAppearTests` cover it.
- **On screen during a meeting:** captures stamped with the meeting id (plus unstamped legacy captures inside the call) become `MeetingScreenContext`: the Summary tab lists documents, pages, and windows that appeared (excluding the call itself), each able to play from its first appearance or open the retained capture, and the Transcript tab shows the window on screen at the playhead. Titles, document names, and URLs only; retention applies.
- **Player:** mic + system tracks play in sync (shared device-time anchor). One slim bar under the meeting's header stays in view on every tab: play/pause, who is speaking and the line at the playhead, **Show in transcript**, the waveform, time and speed. A ▶ time on a decision, action or transcript line plays from there in place; the currently-playing transcript line is highlighted.
- **Meeting page:** a compact header carries the one title, a date line (date, length, app) and who spoke; the tabs are Summary, Transcript and Notes. Summary leads with the outcome: a bulleted recap (whole points expand after five), decisions, actions split into **For you** and **For others**, open questions, and the full notes behind **Full summary**. Find-in-page opens the full notes automatically when needed. Processing badges remain on library rows; failures expose a conditional retry banner. The toolbar keeps **Ask about this meeting**, a **Share** menu with every copy and export, and a short **…** menu.
- **Speakers, owners and refresh:** unnamed call voices show in the header with **Name N speakers…**, which opens one sheet: bounded excerpts (at most 12 seconds), a sample quote, speech time, invited names, or a typed one; a voice recognized from earlier meetings is offered already picked and is saved only with the sheet. Each action owned by someone else carries its owner as a menu (you, this meeting's speakers, invited people, no owner), amber while the owner is missing or still an unnamed voice. When a speaker change archives the old extraction, the Summary keeps showing those decisions and actions, owners stay correctable, and a banner offers **Refresh summary** with the selected inference destination; archived actions stay out of the current action index until refresh. Saved action corrections remain separate from regenerated extraction. Mixed or unresolved voices are never silently named.
