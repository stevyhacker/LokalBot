# Storylines

Three films were considered for a fresh 30-second demo. A was built.

## A. One decision (built)

One object across apps, told in match cuts on one highlighted sentence. A decision is made on a
call, and the same sentence keeps turning up: in the live transcript, in the notes, in a search,
at the end of a sentence you are typing, in what dictation writes, in an agent's answer, on the
day's timeline. The viewer feels the app holding
on to something for them.

- First two seconds: the app is already recording a call; the camera goes in on the live
  transcript as "The offsite is in Lisbon, May 12 to 14." arrives and is highlighted.
- Beats: call → notes and the cited line → search → autocomplete → dictation → agent → timeline →
  end card. (The first cut, 30 s, had no dictation, timeline or voice.)
- Look: brand slate ground, one screen that every scene plays on, Inter 900 titles, one amber
  highlighter. The ground turns mint for the agent and the end card.
- Sound: a voice that tells it in eight short lines, effects on the visible actions under it; a
  quiet, steady bed would suit it.

## B. Monday, 9:02

A moment in someone's day, in one continuous take. A message arrives: "When's the offsite
again?" The camera stays on one desktop while the person answers it three ways without leaving
what they are doing: a search, a sentence that finishes itself in the reply, an agent that
already knows. Warmer and more human than A, but it needs a believable chat app on screen, which
would have to be someone else's real product or a drawn one.

- First two seconds: a notification slides in over a half-written document.
- Beats: message → search → reply typed with autocomplete → agent drafts the invite → end card.
- Look: a real macOS desktop at working scale, natural light, small titles.
- Sound: room tone, a notification, keys; music only at the end card.

## C. The sentence is the film (the bold one)

Type carries the argument. The decision's words fill the frame as giant kinetic type; each word
opens onto the surface that remembers it, cut on the beat: "Lisbon" becomes the search field,
"May 12" the notes, "to 14." the last word of a typed sentence. Striking, but the product is on
screen for less than half the film, and what convinces here is the real app doing the work.

- First two seconds: "The offsite is in" lands word by word at full width.
- Beats: sentence → three words open into three surfaces → the sentence reassembles → end card.
- Look: two flat colour fields, type at 60 to 90% of the frame width, hard cuts.
- Sound: a percussive track; every cut on a beat.
